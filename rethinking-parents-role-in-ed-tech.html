Initial brief

Design a dashboard interface that displays various parameters, including monthly progress reports, journey updates, and performance metrics, and how they are affecting the student's school marks. Parents should be able to see the rigour of the progress provided, as compared to a neighbourhood teacher.

  • The dashboard should also show what activities the child is currently engaged in.
  • The benefits they receive from BYJU’S
  • Tasks they need to complete
  • And also feedback provided by tutors.
A little back story...

Re-Imagining Education Post Pandemic

In early 2022, BYJU’S focused on parents as the shift to online learning tested all aspects of education. Instruction format, attendance, evaluation, technology, and human interaction were re-imagined, with some changes likely to last.

The problem

Why did BYJU’S reconsider the parents’ role??? 🧐

The data showed a significant decline in tier 2 and tier 3 cities as education shifted online due to lack of awareness and trust. Teachers struggled to gauge student comprehension, and parents doubted the quality of online education.

The goal

So, the vision shifted!! The new goal was to enhance the experience of parents, as they are the ultimate decision makers. We need to be answerable to parents!! 😎

Some important KPIs are as below

  • Building trust by making the journey transparent and intuitive.
  • Student retention rate improvement.
  • Simplifying parent-tutor communication to make them feel empowered.
Design process

The process we followed as a team for a better outcome:

  1. Requirement gathering
  2. Competitor analysis
  3. User interviews

  5. Convert our insights to design concepts
  6. Finalise the features & discuss mocks

Understanding the problem statement

The initial step was to identify the important questions that hindered parents from effectively monitoring their child's progress, accessing relevant educational resources, communicating with teachers, and participating actively in their child's learning experience.

Competitor analysis

Going through competitors’ websites

Focus:

  • — How they communicate with the target users
  • — What features they focus on
  • — Visual communication
  • — What are the concerns of potential customers
  • — What features they mainly focus on

Direct competitors are Khan Academy, Extramarks, Toddle and Mindspark.

Competitors seemed to be oriented in the same way. Most of them haven’t provided much value to parents; everyone was very focused on the child. Looking at the market provided me with some interesting insights, but also revealed some drawbacks.

User research

We began by identifying our potential users. The target demographic is people in the 30 to 50 age range from tier 2 and tier 3 cities of India. In the initial phase, we were trying to understand the user's issues in terms of what the parents are specifically looking to learn about their children and why they are not feeling confident regarding online education.

Defining the user types

In this phase, we aimed to understand various user segments and their lifestyles. We explored where our solution could fit into their lives, assessing whether they perceive it as a valuable solution or just another additional task.

Concerned Parents

Parents who are always concerned about their child's progress.

Busy Working Parents

Parents who do not have much time to concentrate on their child's academics and want quick updates on every activity.

Enthusiastic Parents

Parents who are interested in supplementing their child's traditional education with additional learning resources and tools.

Helicopter Parents

A helicopter parent is a parent who pays extremely close attention to a child's experiences and problems, particularly at educational institutions.

Key Insights from research

Recognising the Need for Parental Involvement:

Research revealed a strong need to involve parents in edtech, as many are becoming at-home educators and potential users of educational technology.

64%

of parents acknowledge that tech integration has transformed the way of learning!

78%

take EdTech as a medium that is the best choice for practical learning and effective results.

88%

have noticed that their child is performing great in complex subjects with the help of EdTech.

Need for an Easy-to-Use Platform:

In tier 2 and 3 cities, there's a lack of parental involvement and trust in digital education. Parents in tier 1, with their busy lifestyles, need a simple platform to quickly access their child’s educational data.

Interest in Interactive Learning:

Parents are interested in more than just subject marks; they value interactive and experiential learning that also focuses on behavioural development, and the good news is that BYJU’S was already doing this.

Trust in Online Education with Human Interventions:

Parents want to be informed about their child's performance, class attendance, and progress. They need assurance that the edtech platform is effective, often requiring human interventions to build trust.

Desire for Better Communication:

Parents seek better ways to communicate in this digital world. They want to feel part of their child's educational journey and have a say in it. Providing platforms for feedback and active engagement can make parents feel more involved and valued.

Refined problem statement

Parents need a comprehensive digital platform that provides them with detailed insights and fosters active engagement in their child's education. This platform should enable parents to track their child's progress, understand their learning patterns, and feel assured of the effectiveness of the educational technology, ultimately making them valued partners in their child's learning journey.

Experience design

After identifying the pain points, we started ideating about the structure of the app and how to address the questions and concerns of parents, including which parameters to consider, what information parents expect to know, and when and how we should update them on the whereabouts of their child.

Features for MVP version

Notice Board

To give the appearance of a physical notice board informing them of their child's whereabouts, as well as the information that requires parental involvement.

Performance dashboard

One widget will be on the home page for a quick view of performance, and there will also be a dedicated page for all the detailed views where parents can see various metrics such as average test score, average time spent, topics learned/revised, teacher feedback, and class rank.

Study plan

Parents can view their child's personalised study plan, which was created based on their child's strengths and weaknesses. Classes, homework, and self-study materials for each subject were designed to focus on the child's ability and areas for improvement.

Help Centre

The help centre allows parents to quickly find solutions to various issues through a 24/7 chatbot. To provide user-centric assistance, FAQs and queries are customised based on the cohort and user profile.

Know our programme

A quick guide to help the parents with the different features of Parent Zone. Description of various products/initiatives by BYJU’S. Also special highlights about any new feature.

Study bites

Parents can see their child's small achievements in their daily activities, in image or PDF format.

Information architecture

We started by creating the IA for version 1, and then we cut a few features after consulting with the product manager and tech team. Then we created the final IA for our MVP version. We wanted to make it as simple as possible for the user because we were targeting tier 2 and tier 3 cities.

Visual Design

For visual design, we used BYJU’S Design Library, supplemented by custom elements whenever we needed to create something specific to our case.

Home page

All the crucial data on children that parents require will be available on the home page. Quickly understandable information so that parents won't need to spend a lot of time on the app.

Performance

Status of their child's performance, as well as their class and pan-India rank, will be displayed. All performance metrics such as test scores, time spent, topics mastered in each subject, and weak topics will be highlighted, along with their teacher’s feedback.

Subjectwise performance view
Study plan

Parents can view and download their child's individualised study plan. They can see the status of each task's completion. They can also toggle between daily, weekly, and monthly views to see what classes, homework, and self-study materials their child has received.

Help centre

We have created a comprehensive help centre for parents, where they can easily find answers to any questions. There is no need to call an agent, as they can find solutions to problems across all categories directly within the help centre.

Parental control

Parents can regulate how they want their child to spend their time on the device, by setting up limits for individual apps. Parents can uninstall or hide particular apps, and block websites by simply entering their URLs.

My takeaways

- Identify the user's real problem

The BYJU’S team came to us with a Product Requirements Document that listed all the features they wanted. Before they had decided their target audience, they had already planned what the app would require. My team and I did our best to communicate the importance of research and insights in order to inform product decisions and were able to create a more successful final product.

- Power of the physical system

I always start by thinking about the physical experience first before going into a digital one. Thinking about what we are used to and applying it to digital gives an illusion of realism.

- Don't underestimate yourself

Collaborating with multiple teams, I had to constantly coordinate between Developers and PMs for data asks and to make sure my design was feasible. Balancing business and user needs. The project pushed me to brainstorm ways in which we could incorporate the business needs while not taking away from a smooth experience.
